<section id="overview">
<span class="eyebrow">01 — Overview</span>
<h2>Bring everyday objects into robotics simulation</h2>

I co-created Kamino at **Cal Hacks 13.0**. Preparing a real object for simulation requires more
than a 3D mesh: scale, orientation, mass, collision geometry, and contact properties all affect
how it behaves. Our project brings reconstruction and editable physics into a workflow that
robotics builders can inspect, correct, and export.

The team combined a photo-to-simulation application with an SO-101 robot-arm demonstration,
exploring the practical differences between a simulated scene and physical hardware.
</section>

<section id="workflow">
<span class="eyebrow">02 — Workflow</span>
<h2>Reconstruct, review, export</h2>

<div class="role">
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Capture</span>Upload one to four object photos. Meshy generates a reconstruction from the supplied views.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Review</span>Inspect orientation and set a reference length. Review editable physical properties, with Gemini suggestions distinguished from measurements.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Simulate</span>Export a portable MuJoCo scene with local assets, assumptions, and a validation report. Revise inputs while retaining earlier exports.</div>
</div>

The application uses **Python / FastAPI**, **TypeScript / Three.js**, and **MuJoCo**.
Reconstruction quality, estimated properties, and collision approximations remain important
limits: a scene that runs successfully still needs review for physical fidelity.
</section>

<section id="robotics">
<span class="eyebrow">03 — Robotics demonstration</span>
<h2>Connecting simulation work to a physical arm</h2>

Our team worked with an **SO-101 leader/follower arm** and a charger-transfer task.
The development workflow involved leader-recorded motion, simulation review and adjustments,
and physical trials. Moving between simulation and hardware made calibration, positioning,
and contact behavior concrete engineering challenges.

<Fig src={img('kamino/demo-poster.png')} alt="Kamino demonstration with a MuJoCo robot-arm replay beside a physical SO-101 arm and charger" contain
  label="Simulation and hardware" caption="Still from the team's demonstration. The simulation uses recorded arm motion with scripted charger pickup and release; the hardware footage is a separate recording. The comparison does not demonstrate a physics-driven simulated grasp." />

The repository documents the available replay tools and demonstration evidence. Precise
calibration and reliable contact-rich manipulation remain ongoing work; the hosted photo
workspace does not execute robot hardware.
</section>

<section id="team">
<span class="eyebrow">04 — Collaboration</span>
<h2>Built with a multidisciplinary team</h2>

Kamino was created by **Johaan Mannanal, Atul Modur, Srijan Kumbam, and Yashwanth Piratla**.
Atul's Figma design and prototype informed the interface. The hardware and simulation work
builds on Hugging Face LeRobot, The Robot Studio's SO-101 design, and MuJoCo Menagerie.
