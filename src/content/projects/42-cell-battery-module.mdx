<section id="overview">
<span class="eyebrow">01 — Overview</span>
<h2>Packaging 42 cells as one mechanical system</h2>

I designed a **42-cell, 14S3P battery module in SolidWorks**, with a nominal voltage of
**50.4 V** and capacity of **14.85 Ah**. The work connected cell spacing and retention with
structural loading, electrical insulation, BMS / Vsense integration, and practical assembly.
The main tradeoff was fitting cells, supports, and electronics into a compact module while
preserving clearance and a defined load path.

<div class="note">Project scope: CAD design, engineering calculations, and structural FEA.
The module was not physically manufactured or tested.</div>
</section>

<section id="requirements">
<span class="eyebrow">02 — Requirements & constraints</span>
<h2>Electrical architecture sets the mechanical envelope</h2>

<div class="role">
  <div><span class="eyebrow">42 cells · 14S3P</span>Fourteen series groups, with three cells in parallel per group.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">50.4 V · 14.85 Ah</span>Nominal module ratings; 58.8 V at full charge.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">40g / 20g loading</span>40g longitudinal and lateral cases; 20g vertical case.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Cell spacing</span>6 mm vertical cell-to-cell gap; 22.0 mm pockets for a 21.7 mm maximum cell diameter.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Electrical insulation</span>Separate cells and electronics from conductive structure; preserve connector and wire clearances.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Manufacturing</span>ShopBot-machined plates and proposed PC-FR FDM parts, with accessible mechanical joints.</div>
</div>

<section id="design">
<span class="eyebrow">03 — System / CAD design</span>
<h2>Cell retention, structure, and electronics in one stack</h2>

The assembly uses cell-locating plates, a surrounding cage, a base, insulation layers, and
fastened interfaces. These components establish cell position and connect the internal assembly
to the module mounts. Packaging the BMS and Vsense hardware alongside the cells required
space for the boards, connectors, wiring, and mechanical attachment.

<section id="spacing">
<span class="eyebrow">04 — Cell spacing optimization</span>
<h2>Balancing clearance, cooling space, and packaging</h2>

I selected a **6 mm vertical cell gap**, using published spacing research as a design reference.
In that study, increasing the gap from 3 to 6 mm increased mass flow from 0.040 to 0.045 kg/s
and reduced maximum cell temperature from 46.9 to 41.0 °C. A 9 mm gap reached 39.0 °C,
illustrating the smaller additional thermal benefit of using more space.

<Fig src={img('battery/battery-spacing-research.png')} alt="Cell-spacing reference comparing 3, 6, and 9 mm gaps, alongside published CFD figures and this project's CAD clearance parameters" contain
  label="Published research reference" caption="CFD figure and thermal values: Francesco Leotta, Politecnico di Torino (2020), §4.5, Figure 4.13 and Table 4.1. These are results for the researcher's configuration, not simulations or tests of this module." />

The CAD uses **22.0 mm pockets** around a **21.7 mm maximum cell diameter**, leaving
**0.30 mm diametral clearance** for insertion. Pocket clearance and the 6 mm inter-cell gap
serve different purposes: one accommodates fit, while the other reserves space between cells.

<p class="small">Reference: Francesco Leotta (2020),
<a href="https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/15660/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Design of a battery pack for a Formula SAE racing car</a>,
Politecnico di Torino, §4.5, pp. 48–51. The published thermal results informed spacing selection;
they do not establish this module's thermal performance.</p>
</section>

<section id="manufacturing">
<span class="eyebrow">08 — Manufacturing considerations</span>
<h2>Match the process to the part</h2>

<div class="role">
  <div><span class="eyebrow">ShopBot-machined plates</span>Planar plates, repeated holes, and accessible perimeter features suit a routed machining workflow.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">PC-FR FDM parts</span>The presentation proposes flame-retardant polycarbonate printing for the remaining custom parts.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Cell insertion</span>Defined pocket clearance and separate retaining parts support a staged assembly sequence.</div>
  <div><span class="eyebrow">Assembly & insulation</span>Separate layers and accessible joints allow insulation and electronics to be installed within the structural stack.</div>
</div>

The cell-locating and insulation views above show the repeated plate geometry; the exploded
assembly shows the proposed insertion and fastening sequence. Separating planar plates from
printed geometry keeps each part suited to its intended process. Manufacturing tolerances,
printed-part properties, and joint retention would need verification before fabrication.
</section>

<section id="final">
<span class="eyebrow">09 — Final design</span>
<h2>A complete module-level CAD design</h2>

The final assembly combines the **42-cell 14S3P array**, a cage and base evaluated at **20g and
40g**, cell-locating plates, electrical insulation, and packaged BMS / Vsense hardware. The main
decisions were selecting 14S3P for the voltage target, reserving 6 mm cell spacing, separating cage
and base load calculations, and planning parts around machining and FDM processes.

<h3>What I learned</h3>

Battery-module design requires mechanical and electrical constraints to be resolved together.
This project developed my approach to FEA-informed structural review, cell fit and spacing,
insulation interfaces, electronics access, and process-aware part design.
</section>
